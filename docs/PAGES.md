## Home

The hero contains these exact sentences, in order:

1. Elenchus is bringing questioning back to the center of thinking.
2. Our inspiration came from Socrates’ habit of pressing a statement until hidden assumptions show.

Keep the Socrates drawing beside the text on wider screens. Do not add a second motto or call to action in the hero.

Follow with two compact sections:

- Method (`id="method"`, section `01`): a short prompt, one supporting sentence, five numbered steps, and one practical note. Define the terms, trace the source, find the assumption, ask what would change your mind, and decide. The short prompt from Ask becomes the Method introduction. Step four already asks what would change your mind, so do not repeat it as a separate closing question.
- Examine (`id="examine"`, section `02`): a short heading and instruction beside an example conversation. Medicine, School, and Family retain the supplied headlines. Each example has four brief exchanges that test the inference behind the headline: the comparison scenario, treatment effects across populations, uptake, and sensitivity for semaglutide; recovery benchmarks, repeated age samples, score distributions, and statistical uncertainty for NAEP; annual event ratios, incomplete lifetime outcomes, equal follow-up, and the changing married population for divorce. Questions should expose consequential assumptions rather than ask the reader to recall a fact. The opening headline links to the primary reports supporting the replies. Each example initially shows only its headline and sources; the first prepared question stays in the composer until the reader sends it. Activating the send-style composer appends the question immediately, shows a typing indicator for 800 milliseconds, then uncovers each wrapped line from left to right through a soft edge, continuing in reading order over 2600 milliseconds. The full reply reserves space during the pause, and its lines stay still during the reveal. Send and Previous remain unavailable until the reply finishes; selecting or reselecting an example cancels the pause or reveal. Reduced-motion readers receive the complete reply after the pause. Screen readers hear a short status during the reveal and the full reply once it is complete. Previous removes the latest exchange; disable controls at the bounds. Selecting an example resets to its headline with no questions sent. Previous can return to that starting state, and the counter shows how many of the four questions have been sent. Use a slightly wider desktop panel and a bounded message area of 38rem on desktop and 32rem on phones. Keep its history scrollable by mouse and keyboard, and appended exchanges in view. The examples teach how to examine a claim; they do not provide personal medical advice or score the reader.

Remove the separate Claim section and repeated full-size headings and closing blocks. There is no input field, form, pricing, biography, or capture flow.
