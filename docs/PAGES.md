Follow with two compact sections:

- Examine (`id="examine"`): a short heading and instruction beside an example conversation. Medicine, School, and Family retain the supplied headlines. Each example has four brief exchanges that test the inference behind the headline: the comparison scenario, treatment effects across populations, uptake, and sensitivity for semaglutide; recovery benchmarks, repeated age samples, score distributions, and statistical uncertainty for NAEP; annual event ratios, incomplete lifetime outcomes, equal follow-up, and the changing married population for divorce. Questions should expose consequential assumptions rather than ask the reader to recall a fact. The opening headline links to the primary reports supporting the replies. Each example initially shows only its headline and sources; the first prepared question stays in the composer until the reader sends it. Activating the send-style composer appends the question immediately, shows a typing indicator for 800 milliseconds, then uncovers the reply through a soft rolling edge over 2600 milliseconds. The full reply reserves space during the pause, and its lines stay still during the reveal. Send and Previous remain unavailable until the reply finishes; selecting or reselecting an example cancels the pause or reveal. Reduced-motion readers receive the complete reply after the pause. Screen readers hear a short status during the reveal and the full reply once it is complete. Previous removes the latest exchange; disable controls at the bounds. Selecting an example resets to its headline with no questions sent. Previous can return to that starting state, and the counter shows how many of the four questions have been sent. Use a slightly wider desktop panel and a bounded message area of 38rem on desktop and 32rem on phones. Keep its history scrollable by mouse and keyboard, and appended exchanges in view. The examples teach how to examine a claim; they do not provide personal medical advice or score the reader.
